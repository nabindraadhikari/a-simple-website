My Action History & Transformation

Originally, my bowling style was built around an aggressive, high-impact side-on action modeled after Brett Lee—relying on a strong chest drive, deep hip-shoulder separation, and a long bound. However, after suffering a devastating injury, I was forced to completely overhaul my technique for physical longevity. I transitioned into a highly efficient, front-on fast bowling action modeled after Jofra Archer. This shift reduced spinal twisting while enabling me to generate rapid pace through effortless rhythm, smooth hip drive, high release, and a straight follow-through.

Pace Bowling Reference Models

Brett Lee (Previous Blueprint)

Classical Side-On Power: Heavy chest drive, wide stance, and back-foot load that required massive torque on the spine.

Jofra Archer (Current Action Model)

Front-On Efficiency: Smooth upright gather, high wrist position at release, rapid hip rotation, and a straight, momentum-preserving follow-through.

Top-to-Bottom Action Breakdown

1. Head & Eyes Alignment

Head is kept still and facing directly towards the target through gather and release. The front-on perspective gives a clear, unobstructed view over the front arm to the batsman.

2. High Wrist & High Release Point

At the point of release, the bowling arm reaches near-maximum vertical extension. The wrist remains high and rigid behind the seam, maximizing bounce and seam position just like Jofra Archer.

3. Core & Front-On Hip Rotation

Instead of twisting heavily at the waist, the hips drive directly through the crease in alignment with the target. Active hip rotation propels the upper body forward without putting twisting stress on the back.

4. Braced Base & Straight Fall-Through

The front leg plants firmly to absorb ground impact. Upon ball release, momentum stays aligned down the center of the pitch, allowing a perfectly straight, natural follow-through without veering off-line.
